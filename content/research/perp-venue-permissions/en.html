Trace a credential through the funds flow

Automated execution relies on delegated signing. We start with one compromised production credential and ask four questions. Which orders can it place or cancel? Which balances can it use as collateral? Can a transfer or share redemption change who benefits? If the venue or bridge stops, who has the state and authority needed to exit?

Those questions point to different controls. An order signer can consume equity through adverse fills, canceled protective orders, or increased leverage. A transfer review must follow the ultimate beneficiary. Exit depends on the bridge, available state and administrative permissions. An IP allowlist limits where a credential can be used. The approved host still belongs in the compromise scenario.

On November 14, HyprEarn said it was investigating reports of unauthorized trades on a small number of Hyperliquid accounts that had approved its agent wallet. It disabled old agents on its side and asked users to revoke the remaining approvals on Hyperliquid. For an integration, the test is whether the venue still accepts the old signer. Arcus’s bridge intervened before the manual pause

Arcus reports that at about 22:07 ET on November 9, an attacker inflated an account balance via transfers between its own subaccounts. Three minutes later, the bridge rate limit held back roughly $3 million in additional withdrawal requests. Those requests were never paid and were subsequently reversed. Arcus paused platform-wide withdrawals at 23:07. By then 2,234,391 USDG had been withdrawn on-chain. Arcus says its treasury covered the shortfall. A treasury top-up and a recovery from the attacker represent different cash flows. Arcus incident report

Let C be the opening allowance, r the refill per minute and D(t) the amount requested by minute t. If every payout passes through the same limit and administrators cannot bypass it, cumulative releases are bounded by min(D(t), C + r × t). An opening allowance of 2 million and a refill of 0.25 million per minute allow at most 4 million over eight minutes. An implementation that rejects whole requests can pay out less. An account-by-account limit warrants an additional test for parallel accounts that combine their allowances.

Test withdrawals during a shutdown

A withdrawal available in normal operation and an exit during a shutdown depend on different things. ETHBEAT documents Lighter’s Desert Mode escape path on Ethereum, where users need the relevant state and an exit proof. Its assessment also records an immediate upgrade path, so the escape mechanism does not establish a guaranteed exit window before every upgrade. Lighter contract and exit assessment

ETHBEAT’s GRVT analysis documents off-chain data availability, transaction filtering and an emergency upgrade path. Granular trading permissions still leave two operational questions: who can supply the user state, and how does the withdrawal advance if the venue stops? For the other venues, our scope is the published account and settlement rules. The comparison leaves independent exit unscored where those materials do not establish it. Four acceptance tests

For a trading agent, start with a dedicated signer, read-only market inputs and individually enabled execution permissions. Record the account type, network, client version and date with the results so the same matrix can be rerun after a venue changes its rules.
