How a flash loan turned one repayment into 1.6 ETH

The November 19 Sky repayment reopened stUSDS redemptions. The receipt shows what a flash loan funded and how much the searcher kept after ordering and gas.

Why the discount persisted

At 12:15:23 UTC on November 19, 2026, an Ethereum block included a repayment to Sky’s Lockstake Engine and a successful arbitrage immediately afterward. The secondary market had already put a price on the stUSDS exit. What changed in that block was the amount the protocol could actually redeem. Repayment receipt · Arbitrage receipt

stUSDS supplies risk capital to Sky’s staking-credit system. There are three relevant prices or limits: the vault’s share-to-asset conversion, the amount a holder can redeem at that moment, and the executable secondary-market quote for the full size. The share value measures the accounting claim. maxWithdraw and maxRedeem reflect the amount that can leave under the contract’s rules. Debt and pending liquidation obligations can constrain it even when the vault holds a large USDS balance. A holder who needs immediacy may accept a discount on Curve. Sky product documentation · Vault source

At the end of block 26,449,061, the source analysis reports about 162 million USDS held by the stUSDS contract and about 1.79 million stUSDS shares held by the Curve pool, with zero USDS immediately withdrawable by that holder. Those are historical state observations attributed to the source. The receipts independently establish the repayment and redemptions in the following block. Sky contract

Two adjacent transactions

In block 26,449,062, the Lockstake transaction was at position zero and the arbitrage at position one. The first reduced debt. The second used temporary borrowing, a secondary-market acquisition and vault redemption inside one transaction. In the largest leg, roughly 275,000 USDC of temporary funding ultimately supported the redemption of about 255,869 stUSDS for about 278,095 USDS. The full transaction includes several legs and assets. Summing loans reused across those legs would overstate the funding tied up at any one time. Repayment and logs · Arbitrage and transfers · Morpho Blue contracts

The repayment’s token transfers show 413,660.1706 USDS. The same transaction also moved staking collateral out of the position. The payment and the release of collateral are distinct legs of the state transition. Lockstake transaction logs

The transaction establishes adjacency and ordering. A researcher reconstructing the counterfactual should read maxRedeem for the relevant holder at the preceding block, then compare it with the state immediately after the repayment. The final state of the same block includes the arbitrage and can obscure the brief window. Public execution traces cannot establish when the searcher first saw the repayment or which channel it used to reach the builder.

The source counted seven principal rounds and roughly $401,100 of cumulative borrowing, with about 275,000 USDC in the largest round. Temporary financing removed the need to pre-fund that principal or post collateral. Repayment still had to occur within the transaction, and the sender paid gas. Swap fees were embedded in the realized proceeds.

What an apparent spread actually measures

At an illustrative 1.075 USDS buy price and 1.087 USDS share value, the 0.012 USDS difference looks large. If redeemable capacity is zero, the immediate redemption trade has zero capacity even at that spread. As capacity returns, the executable spread still shrinks with pool depth, stablecoin conversion costs and competition for the first eligible slot.

A useful liquidity view records share value, available redemptions, executable quotes for specified sizes and the state transition that changed capacity. It should also log reverts and dropped submissions separately. A transaction that lands and reverts consumes gas. A private bundle that never lands generates no on-chain charge. The November trace measures one realized trade under one ordering. Ethereum gas accounting · Flashbots bundle documentation
