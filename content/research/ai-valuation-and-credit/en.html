The May source asked how to express a bearish view after missing the rally. The November question starts with a testable distinction. A technology can produce real demand while listed assets reprice the cash flows expected to follow. Read demand, capacity utilization, depreciation and free cash flow together. They respond at different speeds.

Three selloffs with different signatures

Hypotheses and observations that would weaken them
RegimeWhat to look forEvidence against it
Multiple compressionWhat to look forLower equity multiples while cloud guidance and credit spreads remain broadly stableEvidence against itBroader guidance cuts or persistent credit deterioration
Growth shockWhat to look forLower utilization or spending plans, weaker expected earnings and falling long Treasury yieldsEvidence against itSticky inflation or term premium pushes long yields higher
Credit shockWhat to look forWidening high-yield option-adjusted spreads, tighter refinancing and weaker equity breadthEvidence against itSpreads retrace and issuance stays open despite equity losses

Credit spreads and Treasury yields are distinct. A falling Treasury yield can cushion high-yield total returns even as spreads widen. For a credit hypothesis, observe the spread over the government curve and compare it with actual financing access. For a rates hypothesis, compare long nominal yields with real yields and inflation expectations. Equity weakness alone cannot select the regime. Map any hedge to its failure mode

A daily inverse Nasdaq-100 fund such as PSQ resets daily. An index going from 100 to 90 and back to 100 has a flat two-day return. Before fees, a perfectly tracking daily inverse product goes from 100 to 110 and then to about 97.78. A volatility product such as UVXY has a different exposure again. Its daily leveraged benchmark holds short-term VIX futures, whose term structure contributes to multi-day results. PSQ objective · UVXY objective

A hypothetical same-expiry 100/85 put spread bought for a net premium of 4 has a terminal break-even of 96 per underlying unit before costs. With both legs intact, the payoff after premium ranges from a loss of 4 to a gain of 11 at expiry. A slow decline after expiry earns nothing from that spread. Early assignment, contract multipliers, liquidity and costs matter for implementation. A long-duration Treasury exposure instead depends on yields. A put on a high-yield bond fund adds volatility and expiry risk on top of the fund’s credit and rate exposure. OCC options risks · HYG fund holdings and duration

What would make the thesis change

Update three rows after each earnings round. For capacity, track cash and leased capex, delivery timing and forward utilization commentary. For monetization, track segment revenue and margins against the timing of depreciation and cash payments. For market transmission, compare equity revisions, Treasury yield moves and credit spreads over the same dates. Distinguish capex delays caused by supply from cancellations caused by demand.

The October releases give the analysis a dated starting point. A declining equity index is a market observation. A synchronized loss of demand, cash conversion and credit access would be separate evidence. A real hedge is judged by the loss it offsets for its intended horizon and the premium, carry or basis paid for it.
