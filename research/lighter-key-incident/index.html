How a trading key became a withdrawal

The source asks how much damage a key without external withdrawal permission can still do. Assess the permitted trade and transfer actions, the balances exposed through shared margin, and the rules governing the remaining funds during an outage. The six venues make different tradeoffs across these layers. A composite safety score would conceal where each protection applies.

The Ethereum transaction at 10:11 UTC looks like the end of a withdrawal. The decisive change happened upstream. As Lighter reconstructed it, a compromised key could send collateral to a pool owned by the affected account. Another account held shares in the pool and thereby gained a claim on the added collateral. It could redeem those shares and withdraw on its own behalf. The withdrawal kept its owner check all the way to settlement. By then, the economic beneficiary had changed.

That sequence is a practical test for any perpetuals venue. Trace who can sign an action, whose claim grows when it executes, and who can make that claim payable under normal service or during an outage. Each question describes a separate control. A key limited to placing orders can still create substantial losses through fills and liquidation. A proof can attest to a valid state transition under the venue’s rules. A fallback exit is usable only if a trader can obtain the necessary data and submit a claim under the rules that will apply when the exit becomes available.

This report follows the money in Lighter, checks the same boundaries in Hyperliquid, Aster, Variational, GRVT and Arcus, and ends with tests that an integration team or venue can reproduce. Go to the venue matrix · Go to the acceptance tests

The signing failure

Lighter attributed the initial compromise to a trader’s signing code outside its official SDKs. The same private key used a repeated secret value when signing distinct transactions. Each resulting signature remained valid. Together, the public signatures exposed enough information to recover the key. Lighter described its official SDKs and core infrastructure as unaffected. The postmortem does not share the affected signer or sample signatures, so the evidence supports the disclosed failure class without identifying, for example, a particular random generator, concurrent process or restart as the cause. Operator’s technical findings

In the usual additive notation for Schnorr, a signature scalar has the form s = k + e·x, where k is the secret nonce, e is the public challenge for this message and commitment, and x is the private key. If two distinct challenges use the same k, subtracting the equations cancels it. The key then follows from x = (s₁ − s₂) / (e₁ − e₂) in the scalar field. This is an explanation of the failure class using illustrative notation. No affected signature or key is needed to demonstrate it.

Valid signatures establish which credential authorized a transaction. Rollup proofs attest that state transitions followed the implemented rules. A recovered key can still produce a signature that verifies, so a proof can certify a transition that the legitimate operator never intended. That distinction separates the reported signer failure from a break of the signature scheme, the rollup verifier or the L1 wallet. The public-pool detour

The documented secure withdrawal path sends funds to the account owner’s L1 address. A fast withdrawal that can pay another address and a transfer to a different owner require the API authorization plus the owner’s L1 signature. The incident passed through a different boundary. At the time, the account’s own public pool was treated as a same-owner destination for transfers, while separate accounts could hold shares in that pool. The ownership check on the transfer and the entitlement check on the redemption therefore answered different questions. Withdrawal and transfer rules · Operator’s reconstruction

Consider a pool with assets of 100 units, 100 shares outstanding and 25 shares held by a separate account. Before the transfer, the separate account’s claim is 25 units. Moving 900 more units into the pool without a matching change in the share count takes value per share from 1 to 10. The separate account’s claim rises to 250 units, an increase of 225. The pool still owns exactly what its books say it owns, and every share is still valued consistently. What changed was who could redeem that value. This example assumes immediate pro rata redemption with no fees, losses or delays and uses hypothetical amounts.

That gives an audit test. Follow each asset through the entire action sequence, including balance transfers, share issuance or redemption, fees and withdrawal. A rule that examines only the destination account’s owner misses changes in economic entitlement. In this case the authorization check needed to cover the transfer into a vehicle with external claimholders.

Arcus, HyprEarn and Aster

Arcus · a false balance reached a real withdrawal bridge

Arcus reports that around 22:07 Eastern Time on November 9, 2026, an account exploited an internal subaccount transfer bug and recorded a balance larger than the assets it held. At 22:10 the bridge’s on-chain rate limit held approximately $3 million in further requests. Arcus says the requests were never paid and were later reversed. At 23:07 the venue paused withdrawals platform-wide. By then 2,234,391 USDG had been paid on-chain. Arcus says its treasury covered the shortfall. The payout, the requests held by the bridge, and the subsequent treasury funding belong on separate lines of the incident reconciliation. Arcus incident report

The ledger should conserve value when moving assets internally, and the bridge should apply its own payout rules even when the request carries an apparently valid balance. Those controls fail independently. Once a ledger can overstate balances, a downstream limit reduces the possible payout over a specified interval. Repeated attempts, multiple accounts, assets with separate budgets, and a privileged bypass can all change that bound.

Let C equal the opening allowance, r the refill per minute and D(t) the cumulative demand by minute t. The model assumes that every payout passes through one bucket and that no executor can bypass it. Under these assumptions, cumulative payout is at most min(D(t), C + r × t). At eight minutes, the model has $8 million in requests and a $4 million upper bound on payouts. A system that rejects or queues entire requests may pay less. For limits stated in dollars across assets, review the valuation source, stale-price handling, and whether the limit applies at request, reservation, or final settlement.

HyprEarn · turning off an integration leaves a venue-side approval to check

On November 14, HyprEarn said it was investigating reports of unauthorized trades on a small number of Hyperliquid accounts with an approved HyprEarn agent wallet. The notice asked users to remove the old agent at the venue because the approval persisted until removal. The notice did not quantify losses or establish how the signing authority had been used. Reconcile venue-side fills, positions, and approvals before treating an integration as disconnected. Hyperliquid’s permission model can bar an agent wallet from the withdrawal endpoint while still permitting orders that create trading losses. Aster · reported internal transfers with the signing path still unresolved

Coverage on November 16–17 cited a user who attributed seven internal transfers totaling approximately 4.56 million USDT to a browser-created credential labeled aster-desktop. The user dated the transfers to the early hours of November 13, Beijing time. Coverage also reported that the venue was investigating. Inside each venue

For each venue, trace how a trade settles, who can release or redirect collateral, and what a bot’s credential can do. A successful exit through the app only demonstrates the normal path. The profiles below also ask what happens when the gateway, sequencer, validators, or administrators stop cooperating.

Lighter | Validity proofs, a delayed exit and a fast upgrade path

Ethereum contracts hold bridge collateral and verify batches against Lighter’s execution rules. The operator still supplies ordering and inputs, including oracle prices. A valid transition can therefore liquidate a position at an adverse accepted price. If the operator fails to process a priority request submitted on L1, the oldest request can trigger Desert Mode after 14 days. Account holders can then build a proof against the finalized state using the state diffs posted on Ethereum. The delay and the ability to reconstruct a proof both matter to anyone relying on this path. Lighter contract and risk analysis · Lighter whitepaper

The normal upgrade notice is 21 days. The documented 4-of-7 Security Council can cut the notice to zero, and the upgrade must still go through the applicable upgrade authority. A 14-day fallback cannot outrun a zero-delay change. The two guardian paths can pause batch processing and standard withdrawals for up to 24 or 72 hours, while Desert Mode remains available under the analyzed implementation. These pauses are separate from the protocol’s per-transaction-type throttles and from a settlement-wide dollar ceiling. Upgrade gatekeeper and guardians · Transaction-type limits

Lighter’s reference signer serializes and signs transactions locally. Each API signing key has its own nonce sequence. A standard key can trade, change margin or leverage, and send a secure withdrawal back to the account’s original L1 owner. Moving assets to another owner or taking the fast-withdrawal path requires the L1 signature under the documented flow. A signed read token and a maker-only key cover different jobs. On November 16 Lighter also said public-pool transfers would require a wallet signature and placed more granular key permissions, IP controls and per-key limits on its roadmap. Treat the rollout state of those controls as a separate question. API key scope · Transfer routes · Reference signer · November 16 response

Lighter also operates a separate USDG-margined deployment on Robinhood Chain, with a distinct signing domain and contracts. The Ethereum receipt and the specific exit and upgrade timings above refer to the Ethereum deployment. Check the chain, signing domain and settlement addresses before carrying a connector or an emergency runbook between the deployments. Robinhood Chain deployment documentation

The 14-day route describes when the contract can enter Desert Mode. ETHBEAT’s November deployment review reports a replaced DesertVerifier whose circuit sources had yet to be published for that deployment. Independent end-to-end replay of a real exit requires the deployed verifier, data and an available proving workflow. ETHBEAT deployment timeline

Hyperliquid | One execution layer, several ways to custody assets

HyperCore maintains order books, positions, and liquidations under HyperBFT. HyperEVM is its general-purpose execution environment. A balance recorded in HyperCore and the asset backing that balance follow different paths. The October 31 on-chain walkthrough mapped native USDC custody, Circle’s contract and its administrator, and the legacy Arbitrum bridge separately. It reported three of four signatures and an approximately 200-second dispute period for the reviewed bridge path. The bridge checks validator voting power and elapsed time, and Circle can freeze USDC it issues. The walkthrough identified no operator-independent redemption for the custody routes it inspected. Those observations do not describe every asset bridged into Hyperliquid. HyperCore overview · Official bridge source · October 31 contract walkthrough

An approved API wallet signs trading actions using a typed-data envelope over the serialized action hash. Owner-signed actions use a separate payload. The bridge withdrawal request uses the owner's signature, and validators then relay the request to the bridge. An agent can still lose capital through executable orders. Hyperliquid retains a signer-specific nonce window and documents cases in which revocation or expiry can prune nonce history. Generate a new signer when rotating. For the margin at risk, review where the agent is approved and whether the account is in unified or another margin mode. An account-mode choice can change how widely eligible collateral is shared. Signing schemes · Agent wallets and nonces · Exchange actions · Account modes

HIP-3. A builder can launch a perpetuals market by bonding HYPE. It chooses parameters and supplies oracle updates under protocol rules, can halt the market through deployer actions, and can face a slash. Look at the venue, oracle update process, margin mode and deployer separately for each product. HIP-3 · Deployer permissions

HIP-4. Fully collateralized outcome contracts have bounded settlement payoffs. The first mainnet release was limited, with an initial recurring BTC-price market and later contracts using external events. Integrations must distinguish the outcome ID and side from a spot token or a perpetual instrument and identify the rule and authority that resolve that market. Aster | Vault checks, operator permissions and API versions

Aster publishes vault addresses across chains. The October 31 walkthrough found an operator transaction and two authorized signatures on the EVM payout path, and no direct user withdrawal in the vaults it reviewed. The user's request and the vault's authorization are distinct checks. The article links the inspected timelocks and gives a six-hour minimum EVM upgrade delay and then-current hourly caps of $30 million on BNB Chain and Ethereum and $20 million on Arbitrum. The Safe could change roles or caps immediately. The reviewed Solana program had its own immediate 3-of-6 upgrade path. These are dated observations, and the conflicting 24-hour and $1–10 million figures do not establish those deployment settings. Aster contract registry · Vault roles, timelocks and limits

Aster documents fixed-IP binding for API keys. Its API repository distinguishes V1 HMAC authentication from V3 agent-wallet signing with a nonce. The repository dates the end of new V1 key creation to March 25, 2026. A V3 subaccount cannot withdraw externally, and a transfer through the master wallet takes that wallet's signature. Check these boundaries for the specific API version and account type. A browser's delegated signing flow is another credential path with its own server-side checks, so the public trading API guide alone cannot establish its permissions. Variational Omni | Isolated pools, a shared counterparty and a release authority

Omni uses request-for-quote execution. Its liquidity provider OLP is the documented sole market maker and counterparty, and trader and OLP collateral sit in bilateral Arbitrum pools. Pool isolation limits direct accounting effects across counterparties. A common liquidity provider, pricing service and release authority still connect the pools operationally. The October 31 contract walkthrough traced withdrawals to the oracle’s provider role and found no direct user exit in the analyzed pools. Omni's counterparty model · Mainnet contracts · October 31 contract walkthrough

The same snapshot counted 17 provider-role addresses and found an uncapped batch release path and an admin able to pause or change the oracle. The team identifies Fireblocks as part of its deployment and oracle signing setup. A role address and its on-chain permissions do not reveal the number of human approvals, key shares or policy checks behind it. Public market-data rate limits are visible. The public API guide describes authenticated trading access as in development, leaving delegated scope, signing and IP controls unconfirmed for that interface. Signing infrastructure · Public API guide

In a bilateral pool, both sides have a margin requirement. A constraint on OLP can therefore close the user’s position even while the user has positive equity. Omni records this as counterparty liquidation, its form of automatic deleveraging. Separately, Variational describes Pro as an institutional OTC product for configurable derivatives. At the report cutoff its documented entry point was a waitlist. Omni’s custody and credential observations apply to the reviewed Omni deployment. Counterparty liquidation · Pro product documentation

GRVT | Fine-grained trading roles on a validium

GRVT submits validity proofs for its settlement state and keeps the underlying data off-chain. The reviewed deployment uses an L1 transaction filter that can prevent users from enqueuing a forced transaction, and proposer outages can stall withdrawals. The regular upgrade path provides a minimum four-day, three-hour window in the cited deployment analysis. An emergency path can upgrade without delay, and bridge and application roles take different paths. A proof verifies accepted state under the active rules. Recovering funds during a failure also requires the data and access needed to advance the withdrawal. GRVT deployment and contract analysis

Programmatic access uses a session cookie, and order authorization uses the private key of an associated Ethereum address with EIP-712. The help center documents Trade, View and Transfer at the trading-account level alongside funding-account permissions. Withdrawal uses a funding-account signer with a preconfigured destination. GRVT documents optional per-key IP restrictions, but two official articles quote different numerical limits. GRVT also defines on-chain execution delays and ordinary API request throttling. Neither establishes an amount cap that applies across every withdrawal or administrative path. API key roles · IP and session details · Withdrawal flow · Signing and session authentication

Arcus | An emergency exit against the last committed root

The audited EVM rootchain design holds assets in BridgeVault. CheckpointManager queues requests and commits state roots and withdrawals after sidechain validator approval. An aging inbox request can activate emergency settlement and freeze the last committed root. Users need the off-chain leaves and sibling hashes to prove their claims, and pooled margin payouts may be adjusted under the settlement haircut. A live quorum can consume an inbox item without paying it, so an overlooked payout need not make the inbox stale. Correct balances and available proofs remain separate requirements. OpenZeppelin rootchain audit

The audited design permits a quorum to upgrade contracts or reconfigure custody immediately, including during an emergency settlement. An absolute withdrawal pause also reaches the emergency path. Those conclusions cover the audited source and intended role structure. The audit does not establish production role assignments or thresholds. The November 9 incident report says the bridge’s on-chain rate limit held roughly $3 million in pending withdrawals that were later reversed. The report leaves the thresholds and override powers for other paths open. Governance and pause scope · Arcus incident report

Normal withdrawals are wallet-signed with EIP-712. An operator can grant a specific Ed25519 API key withdrawal permission, with binding to the address and subaccount and an owner-only destination. The public API-key creation endpoint does not issue that permission. Arcus also distinguishes typed operations and replay checks, and documents separate same-owner transfers. A withdrawal accepted by the gateway is queued and still needs to be applied. Signing and credential scopes · Withdrawal rules

Five layers of defense

Write controls against specific state changes. An approved trading key answers who signed. An exposure check answers what the accepted order can do. A ledger and bridge check answer whether the resulting balance can be redeemed. Each layer needs an owner and an observable signal that shows when it has failed or intervened.

Individuals · inventory approvals and rehearse revocation

Maintain a record of the venue, account or subaccount, approved signer address or key ID, permission scope, expiry and current operator. Obtain the agent wallet list at the venue where the order is accepted. When an integration is retired or signing is unexpected, disable it, revoke the venue approval, and check open orders, fills and positions from a separate trusted session. Where a venue cancels orders on key revocation, verify this behavior. Where it does not, treat cancellation and revocation as separate operations. Administrator MFA protects administrative entry points. Revoking or rotating a delegated key addresses signing authority already available to the integration. Hyperliquid agent-wallet documentation · NIST key-management guidance

Quant teams and AI integrations · require a policy decision before signing

Keep a worker that consumes market or model data separate from the service that holds signing authority. Pass a typed order intent to the signer and validate the venue, account, market, order type, slippage policy and size. Evaluate exposure against positions plus open and in-flight orders so concurrent requests cannot each consume the full allowance. Require separately authorized workflows for transfers, withdrawal, enabling new markets, or raising budgets. Treat prompts, feeds, messages and webpages as untrusted data even when they came from a familiar integration. Check an AI-generated intent through the same deterministic policy as any other request.

Use the venue’s supported signing library with a pinned, reviewed version and exercise it under parallel workers, restarts, key rotation and network retries. The message nonce used for venue replay protection and the private per-signature nonce used by a signing scheme serve different purposes. A correct result for one provides little assurance about the other. Store signing material in an isolated service where supported, record custody and rotation procedures, and keep sensitive material out of logs and model inputs. Monitor request IDs, signer references, denied policy decisions, nonce errors, and venue fills. Network restrictions can reduce exposure if the venue enforces them across the actual execution routes and the team validates failover.
